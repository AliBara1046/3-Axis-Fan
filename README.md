# 3-Axis-Fan
Handheld fan with a horizontal rotation axis using buttons and servos.


This project honestly started as a stepping stone. I have never committed to building a hardware project, so I knew I couldn't do it alone. I decided to add my friend to the project to help with CAD, since that's easily my weakest link. I can figure out code and wiring, but CAD is a struggle for me as of now, so why not make things easier?

Very recently. I started on Hack Club: Half Life. It's definitely a great program, but it also doesn't feel good as a complete beginner. They start you on custom PCBs and it's hard to work with a custom PCB with ESP32 without even using a breadboard, especially when you are essentially pushed to do 5 of them in a week each.
That's when Torque came in to play. Instead of rushing me into a workflow where I didn't get everything in, I could use a longer time limit in order to do what I really want to use.
Immediately, I called up my friend and asked if he wanted to help me out with this program to help divide the workload and make a better overall product. Our goal was, and still is simple: Make some high quality projects that are good enough to be proud of and also make enough currency to get a 3d printer for more future projects. 
After joining Torque, I jumped right into making the project and immediately got through a bit of my circuit on Kicad. However, I ran into a problem: I have no idea how to solder, or even how to make a half-decent PCB, especially for what materials I have and for what I know how to do. I spent about a week overthinking how to wire the servos too, which didn't help the workflow at all. That lead to another problem: I don't have enough time to work on much more. Junior year of High School demands a lot, and sometimes I just don't want to work on much after the wave of work most days.
For the project itself, my friend and I debated on how to make the fan itself. I wanted to shoot for a fan that rotated on both the X and Y axis, but we clearly don't have the skill for that, so we agreed down to just rotating on the X axis.
